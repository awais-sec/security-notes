# Post-Investigation Procedures and Complaint Cases

## Challan (Police Report)

- The police submit a report to the Magistrate regardless of whether they think the accused is innocent or guilty.
- This report, prepared under **Section 173, Cr.P.C.**, is commonly called a "challan," though that term isn't actually used in the Cr.P.C. itself (it's used in the Police Rules).
- Investigation should finish within **14 days** of the FIR; if not possible, an interim report should be submitted within 3 days of that deadline.

## Duties of Investigating Officer and Magistrate

- The Investigating Officer collects evidence and submits it, plus their report, to the Magistrate.
- The Magistrate decides whether there's enough evidence to proceed: if so, they summon the accused for trial and frame a charge; otherwise the case can be cancelled.

## Types of Final Reports

When an accused isn't sent up for trial:

- **"A" Class**: The case is true but the accused can't be found.
- **"B" Class**: The case is false.
- **"C" Class**: Not enough evidence, or the matter was non-cognizable.

## Cognizance by the Court

**Section 190, Cr.P.C.** lists how a Magistrate can take cognizance of an offense:

- A complaint about facts showing an offense occurred.
- A police officer submitting a written report.
- Someone other than a police officer providing information about an offense.
- The Magistrate's own knowledge or suspicion that an offense was committed.

## Release of Accused

Before a challan is submitted, police can release an accused person who executes a bond (with or without sureties) to appear in court when required (**Section 169, Cr.P.C.**), though this doesn't apply after the challan is submitted. If found innocent and listed in column 2 of the report, they're no longer considered accused unless the court takes cognizance and summons them for trial.

## Supplying Copies to the Accused

The accused must receive copies of witness statements, the police report, and other police documents at least **7 days** before trial (Section 241-A for Magisterial trials, Section 265-C for Sessions trials).

## Proceedings Against Absconders

- If the court finds the accused is absconding, it can issue a proclamation declaring them an offender (**Section 87, Cr.P.C.**).
- The court can order attachment of the accused's property (**Section 88, Cr.P.C.**).
- If absconding with no sign of imminent arrest, the court can examine prosecution witnesses and record their statements without the accused present (**Section 512, Cr.P.C.**).

## Magistrate's Discretion

Under **Section 205, Cr.P.C.**, the Magistrate can allow the accused to appear through counsel rather than in person, in reasonable circumstances.

## Complaint Cases

**Definition**: A complaint is an allegation, oral or written, made to a Magistrate that an offense has been committed, aiming to prompt Cr.P.C. action. A police officer's report isn't considered a complaint.

### Requirements of a Legal Complaint

- Must be an allegation, spoken or written.
- Must state that a person, known or unknown, committed an offense.
- Must be made to a Magistrate.
- Must be made with the intention of prompting Magistrate action.

### Procedure for Offenses Triable by a Magistrate

If the complaint falls under the Magistrate's jurisdiction, the Magistrate will immediately examine the complainant under oath, record the substance of examination in writing (signed by both), and may conduct a preliminary inquiry to determine the allegation's validity.

### Magistrate's Power to Dismiss

**Section 203, Cr.P.C.** grants the authority to dismiss a complaint if, after investigation or inquiry, there isn't sufficient evidence to proceed.

### Procedure for Offenses Triable by a Court of Sessions

For written complaints where the offense falls under Sessions jurisdiction, the Magistrate isn't required to examine the complainant and can send the case directly to the Court of Sessions.

### Complaint Case Priority

If someone is unhappy with police findings from an initial crime report and files a formal criminal complaint, the complaint case is tried first, and proceedings related to the initial police report (challan case) are paused until the complaint case is resolved.
