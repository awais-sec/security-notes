# Anti-Money Laundering Act, 2010

## Definition of a Financial Institution

Broadly, any entity engaging in one or more of:

- Acceptance of deposits and other repayable funds from the public.
- Lending in any form.
- Financial leasing.
- Money or value transfer.
- Issuing and managing means of payment (credit/debit cards, checks, traveler's checks, money orders, bank drafts, electronic money).
- Financial guarantees and commitments.
- Trading in money market instruments, foreign exchange, exchange rate/index instruments, transferable securities, and commodity futures.
- Participation in share issues and related services.
- Individual and collective portfolio management.
- Safekeeping and administration of cash or liquid securities on behalf of others.
- Investing, administering, or managing funds/money on behalf of others.
- Insurance business transactions.
- Money and currency changing.
- Carrying out business as an intermediary.

## Offence of Money Laundering

A person is guilty of money laundering if they:

- Acquire, convert, possess, use, or transfer property, knowing or having reason to believe it's proceeds of crime.
- Conceal or disguise the true nature, origin, location, disposition, movement, or ownership of property that's proceeds of crime.
- Hold or possess property on behalf of another, knowing or having reason to believe it's proceeds of crime.
- Participate in, associate with, conspire to commit, attempt to commit, aid, abet, facilitate, or counsel the commission of the above.

The required knowledge, intent, or purpose can be inferred from factual circumstances. A conviction for the predicate offense isn't required to prove money laundering.

## Punishment

- **Individuals**: Rigorous imprisonment for 1 to 10 years, a fine up to 25 million rupees, and forfeiture of property involved or of corresponding value.
- **Legal persons**: Fine up to 100 million rupees.
- Any director, officer, or employee of a legal person found guilty is punishable as for individuals.

## Financial Monitoring Unit (FMU)

- Established by the Federal Government, may be housed in the SBP or elsewhere in Pakistan.
- Has independent decision-making authority on day-to-day matters.
- Headed by a Director General, a financial sector specialist appointed by the Federal Government in consultation with SBP.

### FMU Functions and Powers

- Receiving STRs and CTRs from reporting entities.
- Analyzing STRs and CTRs and requesting related information.
- Disseminating information to investigating/prosecuting agencies.
- Creating and maintaining a database of STRs and CTRs.
- Cooperating with financial intelligence units of other countries.
- Representing Pakistan in international organizations.
- Requesting feedback from investigating agencies.
- Making regulations for receiving STRs and CTRs.
- Entering into arrangements with domestic agencies.

The FMU may convey regulatory or administrative matters to the relevant body. The Director General may order freezing of property believed to be involved in money laundering for a maximum of **15 days**.

## Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs)

Reporting entities must file STRs with the FMU if they know, suspect, or have reason to suspect a transaction or pattern:

- Involves funds derived from illegal activities, or is intended to hide/disguise proceeds of crime.
- Is designed to evade AML Act requirements.
- Has no apparent lawful purpose.
- Involves financing of terrorism.

STRs must be filed promptly. Government agencies, autonomous bodies, or regulatory authorities may also share intelligence with the FMU. All STRs must be submitted solely and exclusively to the FMU.

## Section 8: Attachment of Property

- An investigating officer may, with the Court's prior permission, provisionally attach property believed involved in money laundering.
- Attachment period: up to **180 days**, extendable by the Court for another 180 days.
- A copy of the order and report is sent to the head of the investigating agency.
- The attachment order ceases after the specified period or the finding under Section 9, whichever is earlier.
- The investigating officer must submit a monthly progress report to the Court.

## Section 9: Investigation Procedures After Attachment or Seizure

Outlines the process following attachment (Section 8) or seizure (Sections 14/15) of suspected property, ensuring the person connected to it can explain its origin and legitimacy.

### Notice to the Person Concerned

- Within 7 days of the attachment/seizure order, the investigating officer must issue notice.
- The notice gives at least **30 days** to respond.
- Requires disclosure of income/earnings/asset sources used to acquire the property, with supporting evidence.
- Requires showing cause why the property shouldn't be declared involved in money laundering and forfeited to the Federal Government.
- If held on behalf of another, or jointly, all relevant parties must be served.

### Investigating Officer's Actions

After the notice period: consider any reply, hear the person, take relevant materials/evidence into account, and record a finding on whether the property is involved. If another person claims the property, they must also be heard.

### Application to the Court

If the officer determines the property is involved, they apply to the Court to confirm the attachment (Section 8 property) or retention (Sections 14/15 seized property). The Court, after hearing all parties, may confirm, retain, or release the property.

### Continuation, Finalization, and Release

- Attachment/seizure continues while related proceedings (predicate offense or money laundering) are ongoing in Court.
- If proven in Court, attachment becomes final and the Court orders forfeiture, transferring ownership to the Federal Government.
- If the person is acquitted at trial, attachment ceases and any income generated during attachment is returned.
- Following forfeiture, the Court orders release of properties not involved in money laundering to their owners.

## Section 9A: Application of Investigation Techniques

Empowers investigating officers to use advanced techniques, with Court permission, to investigate money laundering, predicate offences, and terrorism financing.

### Permitted Techniques

- **Undercover operations**: Officers disguise themselves to gather evidence.
- **Intercepting communications**: Lawful monitoring/recording of calls, emails, or other electronic communications.
- **Assessing computer systems**: Examining data on computers and electronic devices.
- **Controlled delivery**: Allowing illicit shipments (e.g. drugs, money) to pass through under surveillance.

### Time Limit

Techniques must be used within **60 days** of Court permission, extendable by another 60 days if requested in writing and the Court is satisfied with the reasons given.

### Additional Powers and Rules

These powers are in addition to any other powers under other laws. The Federal Government may make rules regulating these investigation procedures.
