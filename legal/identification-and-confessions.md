# Identification, Statements, and Confessions

## Search Warrant

- **For documents or items**: The court can issue a summons (**Section 94, Cr.P.C.**) to the person holding an important document/item, requiring them to bring it to court.
- **House search**: A Magistrate can authorize a police officer above constable rank to search a house with a warrant.
- **Wrongful confinement**: If someone is believed wrongfully confined, the Magistrate can issue a search warrant to recover them; the rescued person must be brought before the Magistrate.

## Recording Statements

- **By police**: Under **Section 160, Cr.P.C.**, police can summon people who may know something about a case. **Section 161, Cr.P.C.** lets investigators record statements in writing, but these aren't signed by the individual.
- **By Magistrate**: **Section 164, Cr.P.C.** allows Magistrates to record witness statements during investigations. These can be made in front of the accused, who can cross-examine the witness; both witness and Magistrate sign the statement.

## Confessions

A confession is when someone willingly admits to facts that prove they committed a crime, either directly or to someone else.

- **Admissibility**: Confessions made to police can't be used as evidence (per **Articles 38 and 39, Qanun-e-Shahadat, 1984**). A Magistrate can record confessions under **Section 164, Cr.P.C.**, and these are admissible.

### Judicial Confessions and Voluntariness

Before recording a confession, the Magistrate must inform the individual they're not obligated to confess, and that any confession can be used as evidence against them. A confession must be made freely, without pressure, fear, or coercion; if voluntariness isn't proven, it can be declared invalid.

### Recording Judicial Confessions: Formalities

- Separate the accused from police custody.
- Ensure no investigating police personnel are present in the court or within sight/hearing of the proceedings.
- Explain to the accused that they're not required to confess, and that a confession can be used against them.
- The Magistrate must be satisfied the confession is made voluntarily.

### Mode of Recording and Non-Compliance

Confessional statements are recorded under **Section 164, Cr.P.C.** **Section 364, Cr.P.C.** prescribes the mode of examining the accused, which also applies to confessions. **Section 533, Cr.P.C.** provides a mechanism for correcting errors from non-compliance with Sections 164 and 364, so justice isn't hindered by procedural errors.

## Identification Parade

- **Legal basis**: Not explicitly mentioned in the Cr.P.C. or QSO 1984, but grounded in **Article 22, QSO 1984**.
- **Purpose**: Confirms that the person or item shown in court is the same one involved in the alleged crime.
- **Necessity**: Essential when a witness only briefly saw the accused and claims they can still recognize them.
- **Fairness**: The prosecution must ensure fairness; the identifying witness shouldn't see the accused after the crime until the parade, which should happen immediately after arrest.

### Precautions and Guidelines

- Conduct the parade in the presence of a Magistrate and two respectable witnesses.
- Keep identifying witnesses separate from each other.
- Carry out identification as soon as possible after arrest.
- Place the suspect among others with similar attire, religion, social status, height, build, structure, and complexion.
