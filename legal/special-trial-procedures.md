# Special Trial Procedures and Magistrate Powers

## Types of Offences and Compounding

Under **Section 345, Cr.P.C.**, two categories of offenses can be compounded:

- **Section 345(1)**: Can be compounded without the court's permission.
- **Section 345(2)**: Requires court permission to be compounded.

## Summary Trials

Under **Section 260, Cr.P.C.**, a Magistrate isn't required to record witness evidence or frame a formal charge in a summary trial, though they may include particulars: serial number, date of offense, and date of the report/complaint.

## Probation

Under **Section 4 of the Ordinance**, a court may discharge a convicted person (punishment of not more than 2 years) after admonition, if probation isn't deemed appropriate, recording reasons in writing.

## Juvenile Offenders

- The **Juvenile Justice System Ordinance 2000** was enacted July 1, 2000.
- A juvenile is a child under **18** at the time of the offense.
- **Section 5**: Mandates separate trials for children and adults.
- **Section 10**: Provides for bail/release of a child; if arrested for a bailable offense, the child must be released on bail with or without surety.

## Offenses Affecting Justice Administration

**Section 195, Cr.P.C.** requires a written complaint from the public servant concerned (or a subordinate public servant) before a court can recognize such offenses; a police report alone isn't sufficient.

## Magistrate Procedures

- If a Magistrate finds a case is more appropriately tried by another Magistrate, they must stop proceedings and forward the case with a report to the Sessions Judge.
- If, during trial, a Magistrate believes the case should go to the Court of Session or High Court, they send the case to the appropriate court.
- A Magistrate can stop a case at any point without judgment, releasing the accused, but must record reasons for the order.

## Compensation and Property

- Magistrates can award expenses or compensation from fines imposed on an offender.
- Compensation can cover prosecution expenses or compensate victims for loss, injury, mental anguish, or psychological damage.
- Police can seize property suspected of being stolen or related to an offense.
- A court may order disposal of such property by destruction, confiscation, or delivery to the rightful person.

## Supervisory Proceedings

A Magistrate may receive directions from the Sessions Judge in the nature of Habeas Corpus (**Section 491, Cr.P.C.**), to conduct raids where someone may be illegally detained.

## Case Diaries, Arguments, Citations, and Short Orders

- Magistrates must maintain and sign daily case diaries.
- Magistrates should include advocates' arguments in their orders and judgments.
- Magistrates must openly cite any case law relied upon, including party names, publication information, and relevant paragraphs.
- Some routine applications may be handled with short orders.

## Final Note

The criminal justice system views criminal acts as harmful to both individuals and society. Justice is the ultimate goal.
