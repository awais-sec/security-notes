# Arrest, Remand, and Bail

## Daily Diaries

**Section 172, Cr.P.C.** requires police officers to maintain daily diaries documenting investigation proceedings: the time information reached the officer, start/end times of the investigation, places visited, and a summary of circumstances discovered.

## Arrest Procedures

- **How arrest is made**: Section 46, Cr.P.C.
- **Arrest without warrant**: Section 54, Cr.P.C. specifies situations where a police officer can arrest without a warrant.
- **Arrest of vagabonds**: Section 55, Cr.P.C., covers arrest by the SHO.
- **Refusal to identify**: Section 57, Cr.P.C., addresses a person who, accused of a non-cognizable offense, refuses to provide their name and residence.
- **Arrest by private persons**: Section 59, Cr.P.C.
- **Arrest by Magistrate**: A Magistrate can arrest or order the arrest of an offender within their jurisdiction, subject to bail provisions.
- **Misconceptions**: It's a misconception that an accused's arrest is mandatory for investigation; an FIR doesn't automatically grant the power to arrest. Rule 26.1 of the Police Rules, 1934 clarifies that arrest authority under Section 54 is permissive, not obligatory. Rule 26.2 suggests deferring arrest if there's no risk of absconding, until the investigation is sufficiently advanced.

### Arrest by private person

**Section 59, Cr.P.C.** allows private individuals to arrest someone they believe has committed a cognizable and non-bailable offense, or who is a proclaimed offender.

## Production Before Magistrate

An arrested individual must be produced before a Magistrate within **24 hours** of arrest, regardless of circumstances. Failure to do so renders their detention illegal.

## Rights of the Accused

**Article 10 of the Constitution of Pakistan, 1973** ensures the right to legal representation and contact with relatives. A lawyer, friend, or relative can challenge detention (remand) orders.

## Remand

Remand means sending a person back into custody to facilitate investigation or trial.

- **Police custody remand**: If investigation isn't complete within 24 hours and the accused's custody is deemed necessary, police custody remand can be requested for up to a total of **15 days**. The Magistrate must justify sending the accused to police custody in their order.
- The Magistrate can't grant police custody remand if the accused is absent.
- If the accused is too ill to be transported (certified by a doctor), the Magistrate may allow detention at their home, hospital, or dispensary.
- Magistrates can grant remand in Sessions-triable cases while assessing whether to transfer the case.
- An accused in judicial custody ordinarily can't be remanded back to police custody; successive remands in different cases are prohibited, though remand is permissible for cases registered at different locations after mandatory formalities.

### Remand considerations by category

- **Bailable offenses**: Remand doesn't apply; accused can't be remanded to police custody.
- **Women**: **Section 167(5), Cr.P.C.** prohibits remanding women to police custody except in murder (qatal) or robbery (dacoity) cases; otherwise they go to judicial custody.
- **Juveniles**: **Section 9(5), Juvenile Justice System Ordinance, 2000**, treats a child under 15 arrested for an offense punishable by less than 10 years as if accused of a bailable offense.
- **Judicial custody**: When the accused can no longer remain in police custody and isn't released on bail, they're placed in judicial custody under **Section 344, Cr.P.C.**
- **Transit remand**: If the accused needs to be produced for an offense committed elsewhere, they're produced before the nearest Magistrate within 24 hours.
- **Discharge of accused**: Under **Sections 63 and 167, Cr.P.C.**, a Magistrate can release an arrested person on bail or bond if the arrest is deemed unjustified, including during a remand hearing.

## Bail

Releasing an arrested and detained person from custody, typically via sureties who guarantee court appearance.

- Bail applications can be made at any stage: investigation, inquiry, or trial.
- Police and Magistrates have concurrent powers to grant bail for bailable offenses.
- **Bailable offenses**: Categorized in the Second Schedule of the Cr.P.C.; addressed by **Section 496**.
- **Non-bailable offenses**: **Section 497, Cr.P.C.** Courts generally release the accused unless there's reason to believe they're guilty of an offense punishable by death, life imprisonment, or 10 years' imprisonment.

### Types of Bail

- **Statutory bail**: Granted if trial delay isn't the accused's fault; doesn't apply if the accused has prior convictions for death/life-imprisonment offenses, is a habitual/dangerous criminal, or is accused of a terrorist act punishable by death or life imprisonment.
- **Case for further inquiry**: Bail may be granted at any stage in non-bailable offenses if there are reasonable grounds for further inquiry [Section 497(2), Cr.P.C.].
- **Pre-arrest bail**: **Section 498, Cr.P.C.** empowers High Courts and Sessions Courts to grant this in extraordinary circumstances (e.g. suspected false implication).
- **Surrender before Magistrate**: In bailable offenses, the accused may surrender and obtain bail, often relying on the word "appears" in Section 496.
- **Protective bail**: Allows approaching courts in other provinces for pre-arrest bail, granted without considering the case's merits.
- **Subsequent bail**: Second or later bail applications should be handled by the same judge as the initial one, even across related cross-cases.

### Bail Procedures and Considerations

- **Surety**: Shouldn't be excessive or create unnecessary impediments to release.
- **Bonds and forfeiture**: **Section 514, Cr.P.C.** covers forfeited bonds and surety death/insolvency.
- **Refusing bail**: Possible in exceptional cases; in-depth evidence examination isn't required at the bail stage.
- **Cancellation of bail**: A High Court, Sessions Court, or the granting court may order arrest and remand for a non-bailable offense [Section 497(5), Cr.P.C.].
- **Bail hearing requirements**: Typically submitted in writing; in non-bailable offenses, the prosecution must be notified and given a chance to argue against release [Section 497, Cr.P.C.].
