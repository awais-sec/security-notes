# Criminal Justice System of Pakistan: Overview

## Basic Laws

- Pakistan Penal Code (PPC)
- Code of Criminal Procedure 1898 (Cr.P.C.)
- Qanun-e-Shahadat Order 1984 (QSO)
- Anti-Corruption Laws
- Anti-Money Laundering Act 2010
- Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act 2016
- National Accountability Ordinance 1999

## Structure

- **Legislature**: Makes the laws.
- **Executive**: Implements the laws.
- **Judiciary**: Interprets the laws.

## Purposes

Prevent crime, protect the lives and property of citizens, and ensure order in society.

## Crime and Criminal Law

- **Law**: A command that creates an obligation for people to act or not act in certain ways.
- **Legislation**: The source of law, consisting of the declaration of legal rules by an authority.
- **Criminal law**: Formally recognized and announced by the State.
- **Crime**: A violation of criminal law.

The **Pakistan Penal Code of 1860** defines criminal offenses and provides punishments for them. It applies to all of Pakistan, every Pakistani citizen regardless of location, and all ships and vessels registered in Pakistan.

## Elements of a Crime

- **Human being**: The crime must be committed by a human. Under **Section 11 PPC**, "person" can include a company, association, or group of people, so corporations can be criminally liable.
- **Mens Rea** (guilty/criminal intent): Required, in principle, since there's no crime without an evil mind. Exceptions exist and are generally listed in the PPC.
- **Actus Reus**: Some illegal act or omission, done with guilty intention. Example: a duly appointed executioner hangs a criminal, that's actus reus (the hanging) without mens rea (no criminal intent).
- **Injury**: Must occur to another person or to society. **Section 44 PPC** defines injury as any harm illegally caused to a person's mind, body, reputation, or property.

## Pakistan's Adversarial System

- **Prosecution vs. Defense**: Compete against each other to prove their case.
- **Prosecutor**: A public official representing the state.
- **Defense counsel**: Represents the accused.
- **Court as referee**: Doesn't investigate or seek the truth itself, acts as a neutral party ensuring a fair process, and decides based on evidence and arguments from both sides.
- **Standard of proof**: The court must be convinced the accused is guilty beyond a reasonable doubt to convict; any reasonable doubt means acquittal.

## Age of Criminal Responsibility

- **Minimum age**: 7 years old.
- **Under 7**: Incapable of committing a crime. **Section 82 PPC** attributes this to being incapable of possessing the necessary mens rea. *(Amended in 2016: this age was raised to under 10 years old.)*
- **Between 7 and 12**: May be capable of committing a crime; **Section 83 PPC** says they may be capable of forming mens rea. Exception: not held responsible if they haven't attained the maturity to judge the nature and consequences of their actions. *(Amended in 2016: this range became 10 to 14 years old.)*

## Components of the Criminal Justice System

1. **Police**: Responsible for catching criminals, concerned with satisfying public opinion that criminals are caught/convicted/sentenced, and work to control crime.
2. **Prosecution and Defense**:
   - **Prosecution**: Acts as intermediary between police and courts.
   - **Defense counsel**: Represents the accused, protects their rights, and is an officer of the court.
3. **Courts**: Supervise police, prosecution, and defense counsel; determine guilt or innocence; impose punishments.

## Court Hierarchy

Established by **Article 175** of the Constitution.

1. **Supreme Court of Pakistan**: Highest court, hears appeals from lower courts, decisions bind all lower courts, comprised of the Chief Justice and other judges, overseen by the Supreme Judicial Council.
2. **High Courts**: Exist in each province and the capital, hear appeals from Sessions Courts and Magistrates, supervise and control lower courts.
3. **Courts of Sessions**: Exist in each district, comprised of the Sessions Judge and Additional Sessions Judges, hear appeals from Magistrates.
4. **Courts of Magistrates**: Two types, Magistrate of the First Class and Magistrate Section 30, handle remands and trials.

## Sentences Magistrates Can Impose

- **Magistrate of the First Class**: Max imprisonment 3 years (can include solitary confinement), max fine 45,000 rupees, can also impose arsh, daman, whipping, etc.
- **Magistrate Section 30**: Can try all offenses except those punishable by death, can impose any sentence allowed by law except death and imprisonment exceeding 7 years.
- **Non-payment of fines**: A Magistrate can impose imprisonment instead, not exceeding 1/4 of the maximum prison term possible for the offense.

## Overview of a Criminal Case: Four Stages

1. **Pre-investigation Stage**: Starts with the First Information Report (FIR).
2. **Investigation Stage**: Police investigate and submit a report to the court.
3. **Inquiry / Pre-trial Stage**: The court examines the police report; if there's enough evidence, it takes cognizance and proceeds to trial.
4. **Trial Stage**: Charge framing, evidence (prosecution presents, then the accused's statement), arguments from both sides, and judgment (acquittal or conviction).
