# National Accountability Ordinance, 1999

## Section 9: Corruption and Corrupt Practices

Defines what actions count as corruption and corrupt practices, covering scenarios where a holder of public office (or any other person) can be deemed to have committed these offenses.

- **Taking bribes**: Accepting or offering any gratification other than legal pay, as a reward for doing/not doing an official act or showing favor/disfavor. Includes accepting or offering a valuable thing without adequate payment from someone involved with their official work.
- **Misappropriation of property**: Dishonestly taking or using for personal gain any property entrusted to them or under their control, including allowing someone else to do so.
- **Obtaining illegal benefits**: Obtaining, through illegal means, property or financial advantage for oneself, spouse, dependents, or any other person.
- **Assets beyond known income**: Having assets far greater than what could reasonably be obtained through known income, with no reasonable explanation.
- **Misuse of authority**: Intentionally misusing authority to gain a monetary benefit or favor for oneself or someone else, only applicable with proof of monetary benefit or asset gain tied to the misuse.
- **Willful default**: Not repaying money owed to a bank, financial institution, or government entity.
- **Cheating the public**: Inducing members of the public to give money by cheating.
- **Criminal breach of trust**: Committing a criminal breach of trust regarding public money; also applies to a banker, merchant, factor, broker, attorney, or agent who commits a criminal breach of trust regarding property entrusted to them.
- **Aiding and abetting**: Helping someone commit any of the above.

## Section 4: Application of the Ordinance

### Who It Applies To

All persons in Pakistan, including those who are or have been in the service of Pakistan.

### Exemptions

- Matters relating to federal, provincial, or local taxation, and transactions covered by government amnesty schemes.
- Decisions made by Federal or Provincial Cabinets, their committees, and certain other high-level councils/boards, unless a public office holder received a monetary gain from the decision.
- Individuals or transactions not connected directly or indirectly with a public office holder, except for certain cheating/criminal-breach-of-trust offenses.
- Procedural lapses in government work or projects, unless there's evidence someone in public office gained a monetary benefit.
- Decisions, advice, reports, or opinions given by a public office holder in the course of duty, unless they gained a monetary benefit.
- Matters decided by a regulatory body established under federal or provincial law.
- Matters where funds don't belong to the appropriate government, with the same cheating/breach-of-trust exceptions as above.

### Transfer of Cases

- All pending cases related to exempted people/transactions are transferred to relevant authorities.
- The NAB chairman reviews these cases and closes those without merit; cases with merit under other laws are sent to relevant authorities.
- Cases can be transferred to appropriate courts or departments.
- All previous final judgments remain in force unless reversed.
- Cases transferred to new courts continue from the stage they were pending.
- All pending inquiries, investigations, trials, and proceedings related to matters not falling under the definition of an offense are transferred to relevant agencies, authorities, departments, or courts.

## Summary

Section 9 defines a range of corrupt practices related to bribery, misuse of power, and illegal accumulation of wealth. Section 4 outlines who and what falls under the ordinance's jurisdiction, and specifies key exemptions.
