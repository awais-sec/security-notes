# FIR and Investigation Powers

## Criminal Investigation

The process of gathering evidence related to a crime.

## First Information Report (FIR)

A report filed under **Section 154, Cr.P.C.**, providing the initial information about a cognizable offense. Its purpose is to initiate criminal proceedings and obtain firsthand information about the crime.

- **SHO duty**: If the information suggests a cognizable offense, the Station House Officer is obligated to register the FIR. This is a statutory duty under Section 154, Cr.P.C.
- **Evidentiary value**: An FIR isn't strong evidence in itself and doesn't provide a complete account of the incident.
- **Registration implication**: Registering a case doesn't automatically imply the accused's guilt.
- **Form and content**: Registered using Form 24.5(1) of the Police Rules 1934. Includes the police station's name, FIR number, date/time of the offense, and a summary of the criminal activity. Contains six columns: reporting date/time, informant/complainant details, offense description, location details, investigation steps, and dispatch information.
- **Second FIR**: Per a 2018 Supreme Court ruling (PLD 2018 Supreme Court 595), a second FIR won't be registered if one already exists for the same incident; instead, a cross-version from the other party is recorded within the same FIR.

## Justice of the Peace Powers

An ex officio Justice of the Peace can direct police authorities in situations like non-registration of cases, transfer of investigations, or police negligence. Additional Sessions Judges (ASJs) serve as Justices of the Peace in their districts. If an SHO refuses to register a case, the complainant can approach the Justice of the Peace for an order to register the FIR.

## Police Investigation Powers

- **Cognizable offenses**: Police have a statutory duty under **Section 154, Cr.P.C.**, and the right under **Section 156(1), Cr.P.C.**, to investigate. An arrest can be made without a warrant.
- **Non-cognizable offenses**: Police need permission from a Magistrate under **Section 155, Cr.P.C.** to investigate.

## Investigation Process

An investigation is the process of collecting evidence, carried out by a police officer or someone authorized by a Magistrate.

- For a non-cognizable offense, once Magistrate permission is obtained, police can investigate it like a cognizable one, but they still can't make arrests without a warrant.
- **Steps**: proceeding to the crime scene, determining facts and circumstances, identifying and capturing the suspect, gathering evidence.

## Optional Investigation

Under **Section 157(b), Cr.P.C.** and **Rule 25.9 of the Police Rules, 1934**, the officer in charge has discretionary power to choose not to investigate less significant cases, and may postpone investigating a cognizable offense if the station is dealing with more critical cases.
