# Fundamentals of Networking

## What is Networking?

In a general sense, networking is the process of building relationships and exchanging ideas with people who share a common interest or profession. This can be formal or informal, and can occur in person or online.

Computer networking specifically refers to the practice of connecting multiple computing devices (like computers, servers, and smartphones) together to enable them to share data and resources by exchanging information through a shared communication medium.

## Foundation of Network Engineering

Network engineering rests on understanding:

- Network topologies
- Protocols like TCP/IP and their functions
- The OSI model
- IP addressing
- Subnetting
- Basic routing and switching mechanisms
- Essential hardware components like routers, switches, and firewalls

These fundamentals are crucial for designing, building, and managing computer networks effectively.

## History of Networking

- Visual systems like semaphore (developed in France in 1791) were early methods to convey messages over distances.
- The invention of the telegraph (1830s) and telephone (1876) marked significant advancements in communication.
- The first computer networks were created in the 1950s for military and research purposes.

## Goals and Challenges of Network Design

- **Connectivity**: allowing two or more points to connect and exchange information, requiring physical connections and the ability to transmit and recognize data.
- **Availability**: ensuring the network is accessible when needed.
- **Reliability**: ensuring consistent and error-free operation.
- **Performance**: achieving desired speed and efficiency.
- **Security**: protecting data and resources from unauthorized access and threats.
- **Scalability**: the ability to expand the network to accommodate growth.
- **Compatibility**: ensuring different hardware and software can work together.
- **Quality of Service (QoS)**: managing network traffic to prioritize certain types of data.

## Basic Network Concepts and Terminology

- **Node**: a connection point or a redistribution point in a network. Examples include computers, servers, and printers.
- **Host**: often used interchangeably with node, can sometimes refer specifically to end-user devices.
- **Media**: the physical pathway for data transmission (e.g., cables, radio waves).
- **Data**: information transmitted over the network.
- **Bit**: the basic unit of data in a digital network (binary 0 or 1).
- **Networking device**: hardware used to facilitate communication (e.g., routers, switches, hubs).

## Connectivity Prerequisites

1. A physical connection between all points on the network.
2. Each system's ability to transmit information to any other system it needs to communicate with.
3. The target system's ability to recognize and act on the received data.

## Physical vs. Logical Networks

- The **physical network** comprises the tangible components like interface cards, hardware, and cables.
- The **logical network** refers to the information carried and the paths it follows, determined by protocols and device capabilities.
- The physical and logical networks cannot be totally separated.
