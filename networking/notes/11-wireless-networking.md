# Wireless Networking

## Introduction to Wireless Technology

- Uses radio waves to transmit data.
- Higher frequencies = faster speeds, shorter range.
- Lower frequencies = slower speeds, longer range.
- 802.11 standards (Wi-Fi) allow wireless communication.

## Wireless Agencies and Standards

- **IEEE**: develops wireless network standards (e.g., 802.11).
- **FCC (Federal Communications Commission)**: regulates wireless devices in the U.S.
- **ETSI (European Telecommunications Standards Institute)**: manages wireless regulations in Europe.
- **Wi-Fi Alliance**: ensures compatibility of Wi-Fi devices through certification.
- **WLANA (Wireless LAN Association)**: provides education on wireless networks.

## Wireless LAN History

| Year | Standard | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1997 | First 802.11 | 2 Mbps |
| 1999 | 802.11a / 802.11b | 54 Mbps at 5 GHz / 11 Mbps at 2.4 GHz |
| 2003 | 802.11g | 54 Mbps, 2.4 GHz, improved performance over 802.11b |
| 2010 | 802.11n | Higher speeds, better range, MIMO |
| 2013 | 802.11ac | Gigabit speeds, higher density modulation, wider channels, more spatial streams, 5 GHz |
| 2019 | 802.11ax (Wi-Fi 6) | Higher efficiency, better performance in dense environments, both 2.4 and 5 GHz bands |

## Wireless Networking Components

- **Wireless Access Points (WAPs)**: connect wireless devices to a wired network, acting as a bridge between Wi-Fi and wired LAN, supporting multiple devices, enhancing coverage, can include security features.
- **Wireless Network Interface Cards (NICs)**: enable Wi-Fi connectivity in devices, convert digital data into radio signals, can be built-in or external (USB, PCIe), essential for wireless communication.
- **Wireless Antennas**: improve signal range and quality. Types include omnidirectional (all directions), directional (focuses signal), Yagi (highly directional, long-range), and parabolic dish (very high-range, point-to-point).
- **Wireless Routers**: connect to the internet and provide Wi-Fi, function as a router, switch, and access point. Dual-band routers support 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz frequencies. Advanced routers offer security features.
- **Wireless Repeaters & Extenders**: extend Wi-Fi range by retransmitting signals (repeaters amplify) or creating new access points (extenders), useful for covering dead zones.
- **Wireless Bridges**: connect two wired networks wirelessly, used to link buildings or remote offices without cable access, act as a point-to-point connection.

## Wireless Network Installation

Requires careful planning. Steps include choosing hardware, performing a site survey to determine coverage areas, configuring security (WPA3 encryption), testing speed and signal strength, and ensuring minimal interference.

## Operating Modes

- **Ad-Hoc Mode**: devices connect directly to each other without a central AP or router, used for temporary file sharing or gaming, limited range and security compared to infrastructure mode.
- **Infrastructure Mode**: devices connect through a centralized access point (AP) which manages communication, used in homes, offices, and enterprises, provides better security and scalability.

## Wireless Controllers

Manage multiple access points in large networks, offering centralized configuration, monitoring, automatic load balancing, and security enforcement, improving performance by managing interference. Used in enterprise-level Wi-Fi.

## Mobile Hotspot

A device or feature that shares mobile internet via Wi-Fi (smartphone, dedicated device, router), used for remote work and temporary access. Security tips include strong passwords, limiting devices, and enabling WPA2/WPA3.

## Signal Degradation

Wireless signals weaken due to distance, physical obstacles (walls, metal), and interference from other Wi-Fi and electronic devices. Solutions include signal boosters/extenders, strategic AP placement, and changing Wi-Fi channels.

## Site Survey

Process to plan and optimize a wireless network. Pre-deployment survey determines optimal AP placement. Post-deployment survey tests network performance. Ensures strong coverage and minimal interference.

## Providing Capacity

High-density areas need multiple APs using non-overlapping channels (e.g., 1, 6, 11 in 2.4 GHz). Lowering AP power can reduce interference, ensuring stable connections in crowded environments.

## Multiple Floors

Multi-story buildings require careful AP placement to avoid interference between floors. Solutions include directional antennas, staggering channels across floors, and coordination with other network admins.

## Location-Based WLAN

Uses wireless positioning for tracking and analytics, requiring high-density AP deployment (closer spacing) at the edges for better coverage, with some APs set in monitoring mode. Used in hospitals, warehouses, and security systems.
