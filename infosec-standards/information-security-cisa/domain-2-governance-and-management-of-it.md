# Domain 2: Governance and Management of IT

## Organizational Structure

A key component of governance, outlining how the IT department is structured, with clear definitions of hierarchy and lines of authority.

**IT governing committees**: Executive-level committees responsible for organization-wide IT issues, covering roles, authority, and membership.

**Segregation of Duties (SoD)**: Prevents errors or misappropriation by ensuring no single person controls all parts of a critical process. Applies both within IT and end-user departments.

**Roles and responsibilities**: Defining these clearly within the IT governance framework keeps IT activity aligned with organizational goals.

### IT Strategy Committee vs. IT Steering Committee

- **IT Strategy Committee**
  - Responsibility: Provides insight and advice to the board on IT topics.
  - Authority: Advises the board and management on IT strategy, current and future strategic issues.
  - Membership: Board members plus specialist non-board members.
- **IT Steering Committee**
  - Responsibility: Decides IT spending levels and allocation, aligns and approves enterprise IT architecture, and other oversight functions.
  - Authority: Assists executives in delivering IT strategy, overseeing IT service delivery, projects, and implementation.
  - Membership: Sponsoring executive, business executives (key users), CIO, and key advisors as needed.

The strategy committee gives high-level guidance; the steering committee handles practical oversight of implementation and delivery.

### Compensating controls for lack of SoD

- **Audit trails**: Detailed logs of sequential activities and changes in systems.
- **Reconciliation**: Regularly comparing data from different sources for consistency and accuracy.
- **Exception reporting**: Reports that flag activity outside normal operational parameters.
- **Transaction logs**: Records of all transactions, reviewable for unauthorized or unusual activity.
- **Supervisory reviews**: Managers/supervisors examine and approve employee work.
- **Independent reviews**: Third-party or independent internal review of control adequacy.

## Enterprise Architecture (EA)

EA documents an organization's IT assets in a structured way, helping management understand and plan IT investment by comparing the current state to an optimized future state.

- **Technology-driven EA**: Clarifies complex technology choices, guiding implementation of solutions.
- **Business-driven EA**: Understands the organization through its core processes, then derives the optimal technology mix to support them.

## Enterprise Risk Management (ERM)

A comprehensive framework for managing organizational risk: identifying, assessing, and responding to potential risks to minimize impact on objectives, aligned with organizational goals and sustainability.

- **Strategic framework**: A structured approach to managing risk across the whole organization.
- **Risk identification**: Recognizing potential risks that could affect the organization.
- **Risk assessment**: Assessing likelihood and impact of identified risks.
- **Risk response**: Developing strategies to address and mitigate risks.

## Maturity Model

Tools used to assess and improve the quality of IT policies and procedures.

- **Focus**: Understanding how well IT-related activities are managed, and where to improve.
- **Usage**: IS auditors use these models to evaluate the maturity of IT governance and management processes.

## Impact of Laws, Regulations, and Industry Standards on IS Audit

- Standards and procedures
- Assignment of responsibility to senior personnel
- Reliable background of staff
- Communication of procedures
- Compliance monitoring and auditing
- Consistent enforcement
- Appropriate response to an offense, and prevention of similar offenses

## IT Management

- **IT Management**: Overseeing all technology resources and related policies/practices, ensuring IT is used in alignment with enterprise strategy and objectives.
- **IT Resource Management**: Investment and allocation practices to get the most value from IT resource investment, including personnel skills, outsourcing, and quantifying nonfinancial benefits.
- **Financial Management**: Budgeting, forecasting, monitoring, and analyzing IT-related financial information; includes "user-pays" schemes where end users are charged for IT services based on a standard formula.
- **IT Service Function Strategies**: Defines which IT functions to outsource, service level requirements, desired provider expertise, due diligence reviews of potential providers, and considerations for contractual/regulatory requirements.

## Outsourcing

Hiring third-party vendors to perform services or produce goods normally handled in-house.

- **Insourced**: Performed within the organization by its own employees.
- **Outsourced**: Performed by external service providers.
- **Hybrid**: A mix of insourced and outsourced.
- **Onsite**: Provided at the organization's own location.
- **Offsite**: Provided at a location different from the organization's site.
- **Offshore**: Provided from a different country, often to reduce cost.

### Advantages

- Cost reduction (lower operational/labor costs)
- Focus on core business (concentrate on main competencies)
- Access to expertise (specialized skills and technology)

### Disadvantages

- Quality risk (compromised work quality)
- Loss of control (less direct oversight)
- Security risk (higher risk of data breach or loss of confidential information)

### Third-party audit reports

- **Assurance**: Independent assessment of the service provider's controls.
- **Compliance**: Verifies adherence to relevant standards and regulations.
- **Risk management**: Identifies and evaluates risks tied to the service provider.

Reports such as SSAE 18 (SOC 1, SOC 2, SOC 3) are how organizations confirm their third-party providers are actually managing and protecting data appropriately, and they inform decisions about outsourcing relationships.
