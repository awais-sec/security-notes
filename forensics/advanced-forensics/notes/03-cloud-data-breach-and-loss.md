# Cloud Data Breach and Loss

Expanded notes on the 36 cloud computing threats listed in [cloud-forensics-overview.md](02-cloud-forensics-overview.md), with a one-line explanation for each.

1. **Data breach/loss**: Unauthorized access to or accidental loss of sensitive data, potentially exposing personal or proprietary information.
2. **Abuse of cloud services**: Malicious use of cloud resources, such as launching cyberattacks, hosting illegal content, or exploiting free tiers.
3. **Insecure interfaces and APIs**: Weaknesses in APIs or user interfaces that allow attackers to gain unauthorized access or manipulate services.
4. **Insufficient due diligence**: Inadequate research or risk assessment before adopting a cloud provider, leading to unforeseen vulnerabilities.
5. **Shared technology issues**: Risks arising from multi-tenant environments where resources are shared, potentially leading to data leakage or interference.
6. **Unknown risk profile**: Lack of visibility into a cloud provider's security posture, leaving hidden vulnerabilities unaddressed.
7. **Inadequate infrastructure design and planning**: Poorly architected cloud systems that fail to account for scalability, redundancy, or security needs.
8. **Conflicts between client hardening procedures and cloud environment**: Misalignment between a client's security practices and the cloud's default settings.
9. **Loss of operational and security logs**: Failure to retain or secure logs, hindering incident response and audit capabilities.
10. **Malicious insiders**: Employees or contractors with access who intentionally harm systems or steal data.
11. **Illegal access to cloud systems**: Unauthorized entry by external hackers exploiting weak authentication or misconfigurations.
12. **Privilege escalation**: Attackers gaining elevated permissions beyond their intended access level.
13. **Loss of business reputation due to co-tenant activities**: Damage to a company's image if a co-tenant engages in illegal or unethical behavior.
14. **Natural disasters**: Events like earthquakes or floods disrupting cloud data centers.
15. **Hardware failure**: Breakdown of physical servers or storage devices affecting service availability.
16. **Supply chain failure**: Disruptions in hardware or software supply chains impacting cloud operations.
17. **Modifying network traffic**: Attackers altering data in transit, such as through man-in-the-middle attacks.
18. **Isolation failure**: Breakdown of virtual or physical separation between tenants, leading to data exposure.
19. **Cloud provider acquisition**: Risks from a provider being acquired, potentially altering service terms or security practices.
20. **Management interface compromise**: Unauthorized access to administrative tools, allowing control over cloud resources.
21. **Network management failure**: Ineffective oversight of network traffic, leading to security gaps.
22. **Authentication attacks**: Exploits like phishing or credential stuffing to gain illegitimate access.
23. **VM-level attacks**: Targeting virtual machines to extract data or disrupt services.
24. **Lock-in**: Dependency on a specific provider's technology, making migration costly or difficult.
25. **Licensing risks**: Issues with software licenses not aligning with cloud usage models.
26. **Loss of governance**: Reduced control over data and processes due to outsourcing to a cloud provider.
27. **Loss of encryption keys**: Compromise or misplacement of keys, rendering encrypted data accessible.
28. **Risk from changes of jurisdiction**: Legal challenges if data is stored in regions with differing regulations.
29. **Undertaking malicious probes or scans**: Unauthorized attempts to identify vulnerabilities through probes or scans.
30. **Theft of computer equipment**: Physical theft of servers or devices containing cloud data.
31. **Cloud service termination or failure**: Sudden shutdown of services by the provider or technical failures.
32. **Subpoena and e-discovery**: Legal requests for data that may conflict with privacy policies.
33. **Improper data handling and disposal**: Insecure management or deletion of data, risking exposure.
34. **Loss or modification of backup data**: Compromise of backups, leading to data loss or tampering.
35. **Compliance risks**: Failure to adhere to industry standards or regulations like GDPR or HIPAA.
36. **Economic Denial of Sustainability (EDoS)**: Attacks that inflate resource usage, driving up costs and potentially bankrupting the user.
