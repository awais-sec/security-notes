# Assignment: Digital Evidence Case Studies

**Prepared by:** Awais Ahmed (Sp-2023/BS DFCS/008)

**Task:** for each category of digital evidence (Direct, Corroborative, Circumstantial), provide two case studies illustrating the type of evidence, the specific digital artifact involved, and how it was used to establish, support, or allow inference of a fact.

## I. Direct Digital Evidence

Direct evidence is evidence that, if believed, proves a fact without the need for inference or presumption. In a digital context, this is often the digital artifact that is the crime itself or directly records the criminal act.

### Case 1: Apple Engineer Trade Secret Theft (Xiaolang Zhang)

- **Type of Digital Evidence Used:** network activity logs / downloaded files
- **Summary:** an engineer resigned to work for a competitor. An investigation revealed a massive, two-year high spike in his network activity in the days before his resignation. The logs and recovered data showed he performed bulk searches and targeted downloads of copious pages of confidential data from secret databases. This digital evidence directly proved the theft of company data.

### Case 2: Child Sexual Abuse Material (CSAM) Possession

- **Type of Digital Evidence Used:** digital images/videos (content data)
- **Summary:** a suspect was charged with crimes related to CSAM. The digital evidence consisted of the actual illegal images and video files found stored on the suspect's computer hard drive. The presence and recovery of these files, in and of themselves, directly proved the element of "possession of illegal content" without requiring further inference about intent or involvement in the creation of the content.

## II. Corroborative Digital Evidence

Corroborative evidence is supplemental evidence that tends to strengthen or confirm other evidence already presented in a case, such as a witness's testimony or a suspect's statement.

### Case 3: Richard Dabate / Fitbit Murder Case

- **Type of Digital Evidence Used:** wearable device data (Fitbit activity log)
- **Summary:** a husband claimed an intruder killed his wife after she returned from the gym, and provided an elaborate timeline. The Fitbit data, which monitored the wife's activity, was used to corroborate or refute his statement. The data showed she was moving inside the house and that her movement stopped one minute before the home alarm was triggered, contradicting the husband's story and thereby corroborating the prosecution's theory of the crime.

### Case 4: Hit-and-Run Vehicle Reconstruction

- **Type of Digital Evidence Used:** vehicle telematics / on-board diagnostics (OBD) data
- **Summary:** a dark SUV fled the scene of a hit-and-run. Police located a vehicle matching the description. Data downloaded from the vehicle's telematics and black box systems confirmed the car's activity: the logs showed the vehicle passed the scene at the approximate time of the crash, the headlights were off, and the car was put into reverse and forward multiple times immediately afterward. This data corroborated eyewitness testimony about the vehicle's characteristics and actions.

## III. Circumstantial Digital Evidence

Circumstantial evidence is evidence that relies on an inference to connect it to a conclusion of fact. It establishes a fact from which the jury or court must logically infer another fact.

### Case 5: Christian Aguilar Murder Case (Pedro Bravo)

- **Type of Digital Evidence Used:** cell phone GPS pings, app cache, and flashlight usage log
- **Summary:** a friend, Pedro Bravo, was charged with the murder of Christian Aguilar. The evidence did not directly show the killing. However, forensic analysis of Bravo's phone showed a Siri search screenshot in the cache asking, "I need to hide my roommate." GPS cell tower pings placed his phone miles away from the city after the disappearance, and logs showed his flashlight app was on for over an hour around that time. These multiple pieces of digital evidence, when pieced together, allowed the jury to infer a plan (the search) and the disposal of a body (the remote location and long flashlight use), linking him circumstantially to the crime.

### Case 6: Matt Baker Murder Case

- **Type of Digital Evidence Used:** internet browser search history
- **Summary:** a Baptist preacher, Matt Baker, claimed his wife committed suicide via overdose. Investigators were suspicious and re-opened the case. They found that in the days before his wife's death, Baker had conducted numerous internet searches on pharmaceutical websites for different drugs that would have fatal consequences upon overdose, including the drug found in her system. This search history did not directly prove he killed her, but it was used as powerful circumstantial evidence to infer premeditation and a motive, contradicting his suicide claim.
